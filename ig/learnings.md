## Current playbook (max 15 rules, each with evidence)
1. **Frame 1 is the hook.** Motion and a recognisable line on screen from the very first frame; no logo, intro or still opening. *Evidence: 83-93 % skip on all 3 Reels (provisional, tested in E1).*
2. **Reels, not info carousels.** *Evidence: four-taps carousel judged at 72 h: reach 1, 0 interactions, vs 14-81 for Reels. Confirmed 26 Nov.*
3. **Every post asks one question answerable in one word or a few seconds**, on screen and in the caption. *Evidence: 0 comments on 5 posts that asked open or long questions; Jordy's rule of 24 Nov.*
4. **Every post must work for strangers.** Most views come from non-followers on the day of the post, so no "as you saw yesterday" without context. *Evidence: 21-23 Nov daily reach split.*
5. **Daily posting stays (Jordy's decision); quality of the first seconds beats more posts.** *Evidence: we post 7/week, above Buffer's 3-5 "sweet spot"; Buffer and Mosseri both say more posts help but quality matters most.*
6. **Always keep 1-2 posts "ready" in the log.** A missed day is a zero-reach day, so a finished post must be waiting for the next run. When a run has time left after the learning part, it prepares the next post as `"status": "ready"`. *Evidence: 24-25 Nov 0 account reach without a post; the ready ask-hq-2 could still go out on 26 Nov.*
8. **More life (Jordy, 27 Nov, twice): meme-native trend formats, no AI faces as the hook, hard cuts on the beat (no whooshes/zooms), content changes every 1-2 s.** See `lure-marketing.md` "MORE LIFE, round 2". *Evidence: Jordy's verdict on posts 1-4 + every static Reel lost 83-93 % in 3 s; tested from daans-pick-2 (E5).*
9. **Check every app claim in the app code before it goes in a plan or a post** (`jordymulch-prog/lure`, `src/app/...`). *Evidence: 28 Nov, plan.md said "live location until you're home"; the code sends a one-time map link.*
7. **The hook line is always on screen as text from 0.0 s,** never only spoken. *Evidence: Mosseri (via nowbam, Nov 2025): about half of people scroll with sound off; a hook can be verbal, visual or audio. Our crew Reels speak the hook, so the text must carry it too.*
